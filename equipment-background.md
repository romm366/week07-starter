FICTIONAL TEACHING CASE. No previous W4 participation or reading is needed.

## The challenge

How can we make it easier for students to borrow campus equipment when they need it? A staffed university desk lends cameras, microphones and tripods. The initial investigation asks what prevents a successful loan, including item availability, collection hours, identification requirements and uncertainty about information. We have not selected a solution.

## What W4 established — and what it did not

W4 used the two fictional sources reproduced in full below to practise separating evidence, assumptions and unanswered questions. Six of twenty logged desk requests did not result in a loan: four involved availability and two missing student ID. That describes requests made during opening hours, not all students or all borrowing attempts. People who never visited were absent from that log. Three separate volunteer accounts raised questions about hours and information; they cannot be linked to the log.

A possible starting assumption is “Item shortages are the main obstacle.” It remains a hypothesis: the records exclude non-visitors and cannot rank all barriers. W7 asks what new interviews and observations add or challenge. Returning students should revisit the evidence, not rely on a remembered answer.

# Fictional equipment-loan source pack
All records below are synthetic teaching examples, not real campus data.

## S1 — Fictional service note, section 1: scope and records
A staffed desk lends cameras, microphones, and tripods. During one fictional
Monday–Friday week it logged 20 equipment requests made at the desk during
opening hours. It did not record people who did not visit the desk.
Fourteen requests resulted in a loan. Six did not. Of those six, four had
no requested item available at the requested time; two could not be
completed because the requester did not have the required student ID.
The log does not record why people needed equipment or what they did next.

## S1 — section 2: information and hours
The desk opens 10:00–16:00 on weekdays. A booking page lists available
categories and the student-ID requirement. Its inventory is updated once
per weekday. The note does not establish whether visitors read the page
or whether displayed availability matched the desk at any particular time.

## S2 — Fictional exploratory conversations, section 1: sampling
A fictional exercise asked three volunteers from a campus media club
about borrowing equipment. They were not sampled from the request log.
Their experiences cannot be matched to the 20 requests. These accounts
cannot establish the prevalence of a problem across campus.

## S2 — section 2: accounts
Volunteer A said: “I wanted to collect a camera after my class at 17:00,
but I did not go to the desk because it would be closed.”
Volunteer B said: “The page showed microphones, but I wasn't sure whether
one would be there when I arrived. I borrowed one from a friend instead.”
Volunteer C said: “I borrowed a tripod successfully. Bringing my student
ID was straightforward.”

## S2 — section 3: unknowns
The exercise did not measure willingness to use extended hours, verify
past inventory displays, or ask volunteers to compare possible solutions.
