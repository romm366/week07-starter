# Fictional university-equipment borrowing research notes

FICTIONAL PRACTICE — constructed teaching data, not real university research.

## Case recap and collection context

A staffed university desk lends cameras, microphones and tripods, weekdays 10:00–16:00. Student ID is required. Its booking page lists categories and the ID requirement; inventory is updated once per weekday, but accuracy at a particular time is not established. Read equipment-background.md for the complete challenge introduction and the two earlier fictional source documents, reproduced within W7. No W4 knowledge is required.

Question: What helps or prevents students borrowing equipment when they need it?
Starting assumption to challenge: item shortages are the main obstacle. The earlier desk log could not capture people who never visited.

These are new fictional encounters in a later teaching week. Eight convenience interviewees were recruited near teaching rooms after classes, not selected from the old desk log or earlier volunteers. All were asked “Tell me about the last time you considered borrowing university equipment.” No price question was asked. These short excerpts are not complete transcripts. The two separate observations are unlinked to interview participants; identifiers do not imply a match to W4 volunteers A–C. S1/S2 stay background, outside this coding/counting dataset.

# Grocery-shopping interviews supplied by the user

These three supplied interviews are separate from the fictional equipment-borrowing practice above. The following are paraphrased interview notes, not verbatim quotations. ## I9 — INT09 — P9 — interview, Grocery shopping; Elisabeth's interview with her mom

Paraphrase: Shopping: Shops at Selver about twice a week. Health matters, but she often chooses discounted products and compares price per kilo. Dairy and bread are familiar products bought out of habit.

Paraphrase: Convenience: When tired or rushed, she makes simple food. She buys chicken nuggets and fries when the children are home alone because they are easy to prepare. Pelmeni are always kept at home for quick meals.

Paraphrase: Labels and sweeteners: Wants less sugar but prefers sugar to artificial sweeteners. Sometimes buys light cheese because of high cholesterol, although she dislikes its taste. She does not regard no added sugar as automatically healthy.

Paraphrase: Trust and package checks: Reports buying dried mangoes with a lot of sugar and seeing bread marketed as yeast-free sourdough with yeast still listed in the ingredients. Does not trust front-of-pack imagery and tries to check the ingredients. Checks the date and shelf life first, but skips ingredients when rushed. Assumes she knows the contents of familiar basic foods such as rye bread and plain yogurt.

Paraphrase: Product task: Ranked Farmi Skyr first, Farmi sugars-from-fruit yogurt second, Tere FIT third, and Farmi cream yogurt fourth, from healthiest to least healthy. Assumed all contained sugar. Buys plain yogurt herself and would not buy Tere FIT because she dislikes sweeteners. The supplied comparison lists sugar per 100 g as FIT 3.9 g, Skyr 9.8 g, sugars-from-fruit yogurt 11.4 g, and cream yogurt 14.6 g. These figures are supplied task material, not independently verified nutrition data.

Paraphrase: Help with choices: Usually chooses plain products, palm-oil-free biscuits, and wholegrain and oat products. Does not check sugar in biscuits. Has seen Nutri-Score at Lidl but is unsure of its accuracy. Wants honest and correct information from stores and believes undesirable ingredients or qualities are hidden.

## I10 — INT10 — P10 — interview, Grocery shopping; Romeo's interview with a health-conscious mom

Paraphrase: Shopping: Shops mainly at Selver and sometimes Rimi, around five times a week. Usually buys what she needs at the moment rather than making one large weekly trip. No specific last shopping trip was described.

Paraphrase: Priorities: Price, taste, habit and health all matter; speed is generally not important. Adds quality as an important factor. Prefers familiar, trusted producers and local products, and usually avoids unknown producers.

Paraphrase: Convenience: When very hungry, tired and rushed at the same time, she is more likely to order through Bolt Food or Wolt. Says this rarely happens and that she usually considers what she eats and buys.

Paraphrase: Label meanings: Understands light as less sugar than the regular version; no added sugar as no extra sugar added, while natural sugars such as fruit sugar may remain; and sugar free as containing no cane sugar, fruit sugar or other natural sugars. These are the participant's interpretations.

Paraphrase: Trust and package checks: Generally trusts labels without checking every detail or comparing several similar products. Trust depends on knowing the producer; gives Tallegg ham with a Fitlap or similar label as an example. Looks early at nutritional information on the back and pays little attention to package design. For unfamiliar products, checks the producer and country of production.

Paraphrase: Product task 1: Chose Coca-Cola Zero as healthier than regular Coca-Cola, mainly because the large Zero Sugar label suggested no sugar. Said the supplied sugar and calorie information per 100 ml matched her expectations. Exact nutrition figures were not included in the interview text.

Paraphrase: Product task 2: Chose plain Greek yogurt over fruit-flavoured Greek yogurt. Believes flavouring often adds jam, fruit preparation or another sweet ingredient rather than just fresh fruit. Views flavoured yogurt as a sweet convenient snack and plain yogurt as a basic ingredient for a healthier meal. Said the nutrition comparison did not surprise her; exact figures were not provided.

Paraphrase: Help with choices: A shopping list prepared beforehand would help her decide faster, avoid random purchases and stick to planned healthier choices.

## I11 — INT11 — P11 — interview, Grocery shopping; Egle's interview with a University of Tartu PhD student

Paraphrase: Shopping: Shops at Coop, Rimi and Selver at least twice a week, usually more often. On the last trip, at a Rimi hypermarket early in the week, planned ahead and bought three different meats for three nights, salad ingredients, essentials and fish to vary the week's proteins.

Paraphrase: Priorities: Price, taste, habit, health and speed all matter depending on circumstances. Speed matters during busy periods and price when financial responsibilities are greater. Usually habit and health matter most; associates healthier food with higher quality and better taste.

Paraphrase: Convenience: Being tired and rushed often leads to fast food. Being hungry tends to mean buying more groceries, but not necessarily less healthy groceries.

Paraphrase: Label meanings: Understands sugar free as no sugar, no added sugar as usually involving fruit sugar, and light as less sugar than the regular product. These are the participant's interpretations.

Paraphrase: Trust and package checks: Checks ingredients. Has bought protein bars marketed as healthy, while doubting that they were truly healthy; thought they might be better than a Snickers bar but now questions that comparison too. Looks at the front label, ingredients and producing country. Almost never reads the product description.

Paraphrase: Product task: Chose Coca-Cola Zero because she thought the quantity of sugar substitutes was lower than the sugar in regular Coca-Cola. Chose kefir bread because of extra calcium and a belief that kefir is good for health. Chose original Philadelphia over the plant-based version because she recalled concerns about sugar in plant-based products and almond products being less healthy than marketed; expressed uncertainty about this recollection. Chose what she understood to be vacuum-sealed corn because she thought the other product sat in sugar. These explanations record her reasoning rather than verified nutrition claims.

Paraphrase: Reaction to task information: Was not very surprised, but pointed out AI-style questions at the end of some comparisons and said both corn products were in water, with no vacuum-sealed option. The product images were absent from the pasted text, so the exact corn comparison cannot be established. The extra product-comparison commentary following the interview is not treated as participant testimony or verified evidence.

Paraphrase: Help with choices: Wants honest marketing. No answer was supplied to the final question asking whether anything important had been missed.
